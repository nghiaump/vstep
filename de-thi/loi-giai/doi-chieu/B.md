## W1: Letter / email

**Structure**
- Informal: Hi/Dear [Name] → greeting + thanks + reason → one paragraph per question (with a reason and an example) → wishes/invitation → "Best wishes," + name [SN].
- Formal: Dear Mr/Ms [Surname] / Dear Sir or Madam → "I am writing regarding…" → request/info 1 → request/info 2 → thanks + contact line → Yours sincerely (named) / Yours faithfully (unnamed) + full name [SN].
- Each point: direct answer → explanation → detail/example [SN].
- (model pattern) 4–6 paragraphs: a 1–2 sentence opener → 1 body paragraph per bullet → a 1–2 sentence close → sign-off [CH].

**Template phrases**
- "About your question on…" / "You asked me about…" [SN]
- "As for…" / "Regarding…" [SN, CH]
- "Personally,…" / "If I were you,…" [SN]
- "Besides,…" / "What's more,…" [SN]
- "Hope to hear from you soon." / "Write back when you can." [SN]
- "I am writing regarding [purpose]." [SN]
- "I would appreciate it if…" / "With regard to…" [SN]
- "Thank you for considering my request." / "Please contact me if further information is required." [SN]
- "It was great to hear from you." [CH]
- "I hope you're doing well. I'm writing to let you know / because…" [CH]
- "How about meeting at [place] at [time]?" [CH]
- "I hope this information is helpful." / "Let me know what you think." [CH]
- "Thank you for your consideration. I look forward to your reply." [CH]
- "please do not hesitate to contact me" [CH]

**Length/time**
- At least 120 words [SN, CH]. The practice target is 150–170 words [SN]. Some CH prompts say "120–150 words".
- About 20 minutes [CH].
- (model pattern) CH letters run 149–200 words, most of them 170–195.

**Tips (do / don't)**
- Answer every bullet/question [SN].
- N questions → consider N body paragraphs (a practice rule, not a hard rule) [SN].
- Choose informal or formal according to the recipient (friend vs organisation) [SN].
- Contractions are fine in informal letters. Formal letters avoid slang and limit contractions [SN]. (model pattern) CH's formal letters have no contractions [CH].
- Don't make the opener longer than the content. Don't sound too formal to a friend. Don't stack stock transitions [SN].
- Formal letters state their purpose clearly, with the tone adjusted to that purpose (complaint, request, application) [SN].
- Respond directly to the prompt's situation [SN].
- Include a greeting, an opener, a closing and a signature [SN].
- Proofread tenses, articles, prepositions and plurals [SN].
- (model pattern) A suggested meeting gives a concrete day/time and place ("9:00 a.m. next Sunday", "sports center in Central Park"). A cancellation goes: apologise → reason → alternative time and place → apologise again [CH].

## W2: Essay

**Structure**
- 4 paragraphs: Introduction → Body 1 → Body 2 → Conclusion [SN, CH outlines].
- Opinion: Intro (paraphrase + concession + position) → B1 the opposing view (1 PEER) → B2 two reasons (2 PEER) → Conclusion (restate + message/recommendation) [SN, CH].
- Discussion + opinion: B1 side A (1 PEER) → B2 side B + your choice (2 PEER) [SN, CH].
- Advantages/disadvantages: B1 disadvantage (1 PEER) → B2 advantages (2 PEER) [SN, CH, VI].
- Cause/solution: B1 causes (2 PEER, or 1–2 [SN]) → B2 solutions matched to the causes (2 PEER) [SN, CH].
- Cause/effect: B1 causes → B2 effects (2 PEER each) [SN].
- Conclusion = Summation + Final Remark, or Restate Position + Recommendation [CH].
- PEER = Point → Explain → Example → Result. Don't write the labels in the essay [SN].

**Template phrases**
- "It is widely acknowledged/believed that…" [CH]
- "People have different opinions on whether…" [CH]
- "While I acknowledge that…, I firmly believe that…" [CH]
- "Although [opposing consideration], I believe [position]." [SN]
- "these drawbacks pale in comparison with the advantages it offers" [CH]
- "This essay will examine the main reasons… and propose two effective solutions." [CH]
- "The following paragraphs will shed light on both perspectives before concluding with my stance." [CH]
- "Admittedly, … One notable drawback is that…" [SN, CH]
- "On the one hand, there are compelling reasons why some people support…" [CH]
- "Nevertheless, I support [position] for two main reasons. First,… Furthermore,…" [SN]
- "Even so, I believe that … provides far more substantial benefits than drawbacks." [CH]
- "To tackle this problem, several effective measures should be implemented." [CH]
- "The primary reason is that…" / "Another convincing argument is that…" [CH]
- "This can be explained by / attributed to the fact that…" [CH]
- "A good example of this is…" / "Take X as an illustration." [CH, VI]
- "As a result / Consequently / Hence," [CH, SN]
- "In conclusion, although…, I remain convinced that…" [CH]
- "Overall, despite [limitation], I believe…" [SN]

**Length/time**
- At least 250 words, about 40 minutes [CH prompts]. SN gives no number ("meet the exam's word count"; quality is not judged by word count).
- (model pattern) CH essays run 276–364 words, most of them 310–340.

**Tips (do / don't)**
- Identify the task type and instruction verbs first (discuss / opinion / causes / solutions / own experience) [SN].
- The thesis answers the question directly and matches the body paragraphs. Don't announce "effects" and then write about causes [SN].
- If the prompt asks for experience, give it its own paragraph (time, context, action, result) [SN].
- Every point needs an explanation, an example and a result, so you argue rather than list [SN, CH, VI].
- 2 strong ideas beat many weak ones [SN].
- Use easy-to-picture examples rather than invented statistics, and tie them to the group in the prompt [SN].
- Don't make the opposing side artificially weak [SN].
- Solutions need an actor and a mechanism, and they must fit the causes [SN].
- An "outweigh" question needs a clear verdict. A plain "adv/disadv" question doesn't have to favour the advantages [SN].
- No body paragraph contradicts the thesis. The conclusion adds no new argument [SN].
- Don't repeat "This is because… For example… As a result…" in every paragraph [SN].
- Check in this order: complete ideas → logic → language → word count [SN].

## S1: Social interaction

**Structure:** Answer → Reason → Detail/Example [SN].

**Template phrases:** "Yes, I do. I find [X] both enjoyable and educational." / "It gives me a break from my routine…" / "Whenever possible, I…" [SN]

**Length/time:** none stated. The sample answers are 2–3 sentences [SN].

**Tips**
- Answer directly in the first sentence [SN].
- Add 1 reason and 1 real or imagined detail [SN].
- Don't turn the answer into an essay. Balance natural speech with completeness [SN].

## S2: Solution discussion

**Structure**
- Identify who is deciding and their goal → pick 2 criteria → defend your choice, with PEER per criterion → reject the other two briefly, using drawbacks tied to the context [SN].
- Frame: choice → "two main reasons" → First → Second → As for B → Regarding C → close [SN].

**Template phrases** [SN]
- "If I were in this situation, I would choose [option]."
- "There are two main reasons for my decision."
- "First, … Second, …"
- "As for [option B], it might [benefit], but [specific limitation]."
- "Regarding [option C], [specific drawback in this context]."
- "For these reasons, [option A] seems the most suitable choice."
- Criteria bank: cost, time, effectiveness, health, social interaction, personal development, long-term benefits, practicality.

**Length/time:** none stated [SN].

**Tips**
- You must give reasons for your choice **and** reasons for rejecting each of the other two [SN §6.1, §9, §11].
- Use 2–3 relevant criteria, not all of them. Too many wastes time [SN].
- Use a criterion's weak side to reject the other options [SN].
- Don't claim an option is cheaper or better without a basis in the prompt [SN].
- Fit the examples to the person in the prompt (not always "students") [SN].
- Speak naturally rather than reciting a written paragraph [SN].

## S3: Topic development

**Structure**
- Direct answer → reason → example → result → (limitation if needed) [SN].
- By question type: "Why important?" → immediate effect + long-term benefit. "Benefits?" → individual + community. "Changed?" → past vs now + cause. "What can be done?" → who + what + expected effect. "Disadvantages?" → limitation + mitigation [SN].

**Template phrases:** "My view is that…" / "One important reason is…" / "For example,…" / "This means that…" / "However,…" [SN]

**Length/time:** longer than Part 1 but straight to the point. No time is given [SN].

**Tips**
- Extend with effects or an extra perspective when needed [SN].
- Develop each idea with PEER [SN].
- **None of the files mentions the mind map, covering all branches, or adding "your own idea".** SN treats Part 3 as a follow-up Q&A only.

## General

- Use PEER for every developed idea, in writing and in speaking [SN, CH, VI].
- Linkers by function [SN]:
  - Starting: First, To begin with
  - Adding: In addition, Furthermore
  - Explaining: This is because, Since
  - Illustrating: For example, To illustrate
  - Result: As a result, Therefore
  - Contrast: However, Nevertheless
  - Concession: Admittedly, While it is true that
  - Concluding: In conclusion, Overall
- Fulfil the task first and worry about vocabulary second. Clear grammar and linking matter more than rare words [SN].
- Templates are a thinking frame, not a script. Adapt the linkers, examples and number of points [SN].
- Don't repeat words or recite formulas mechanically. Make examples fit the topic [SN].
- Self-check on 4 criteria: task fit, coherence, language, naturalness [SN].

## Conflicts / differences between sources
1. **W1 length:** SN's target is 150–170 words, and some CH prompts cap it at 120–150. CH's own models run 149–200 words, past both.
2. **Linker repetition:** SN forbids repeating "This is because… For example… As a result…" in every paragraph. CH essays, CH outlines and VI chains use that exact chain in nearly every body point.
3. **Paragraph count:** SN and the CH outlines prescribe 4 paragraphs. CH's overpopulation essay has 3 body paragraphs, and VI has a "BODY 1–6" set.
4. **Opinion B1:** SN requires an opposing-view paragraph. CH's memory essay agrees fully and has no opposing paragraph.
5. **Adv/disadv verdict:** SN says a plain adv/disadv question needn't favour the advantages. CH uses the "pale in comparison" pro-advantage template even where no opinion is asked (computer technology essay).
6. **Balance:** SN says be fair to both sides. The 1 PEER vs 2 PEER pattern (SN, CH) is lopsided, even in CH's no-opinion "discuss both views" essay.
7. **Formal sign-off:** SN says sincerely when named and faithfully when unnamed. CH's furniture letter is "Dear Ms. Smith … Yours faithfully" (mismatch).
8. **Name after sign-off:** SN's templates include a name. CH's informal models omit it, as the prompts allow.
9. **One paragraph per bullet:** CH sometimes merges bullets (festival letter, hospital letter). SN calls the rule a guideline.
10. **Causes:** CH's brain-drain essay announces "the primary cause" (1 PEER) but concludes with two. The SN/CH outlines expect 2 causes + 2 solutions.
11. **Speaking:** only SN covers it, with no timings for S1–S3 and no S3 mind-map/own-idea rule. No source in this set supports an S3 branch-coverage check.

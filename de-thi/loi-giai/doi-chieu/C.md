# Rulebook C: TỔNG_WRITING + Đề key VSTEP Writing/Speaking

**Sources**
- [TW] `TỔNG_WRITING.txt`: 25 W1 prompts (24 with model letters) and about 140 W2 prompts. Models exist only for advantages/disadvantages essays (about 23) and discuss-both-views essays (8). Agree/disagree and causes/solutions prompts have no models. **No explicit tips.**
- [KW] `Đề_key_VSTEP_WRITING_-_VSTEP_SV.txt`: 7 W1 model letters and about 23 W2 model essays (discuss, causes–solutions, causes–consequences, partly agree). **No explicit tips.**
- [KS] `Đề_key_VSTEP_SPEAKING_-_VSTEP_SV.txt`: S1 question lists and S2 option lists, both without answers, plus 6 S3 model monologues and 3 model follow-up answers.

Rules are from exam prompts (*prompt*) or consistent model-answer patterns (*pattern*).

## W1: Letter / email

**Structure**
- Informal [TW]: "Hi <Name>," → thanks + apology for not writing + "How are things?" → **one paragraph per bullet** (2–4) → fixed closing line → "Looking forward to hearing from you." → "All the best," with no name.
- Formal [TW]: "Dear Mr. X," or "Dear Sir/ Madam," → "in response to your request…" → one paragraph per point, each opened by Concerning / In terms of / Regarding / With regard to → offer of further help → "Yours sincerely," (named reader) or "Yours faithfully," (Sir/Madam).
- Informal [KW]: **one single block paragraph**. "Dear <Name>," → "How are you these days? We haven't seen each other… Hope everything goes well" → "I am writing this letter to…" → the bullets in order (Honestly / Besides / By the way) → "I hope my advice / the information is helpful" → "I look forward to hearing from you…" → "Love," or "Hugs,".
- Formal [KW]: "I hope this letter finds you well." → self-introduction → "I am writing this letter to…" → content → "I look forward to hearing from you as soon as possible." → "Sincerely,".

**Template phrases**
- "Many thanks for your recent letter. I'm sorry I haven't been in touch for such a long time. How are things?" [TW]
- "Your project sounds fascinating!" (react first) / "As for your other question, …" [TW]
- "Anyway, I must go and get on with my studying. Drop me a line when you have time and let me know what you think." [TW]
- "I'm writing in response to your request for information related to…" [TW formal]
- "Concerning… / In terms of… / Regarding… / With regard to…" [TW formal]
- "I hope that I have been of some assistance to you. Please do not hesitate to contact me if you require any further information." [TW formal]
- "How about we meet up at <place> next Saturday at 4:00 PM?" [TW]
- "I am writing this letter to let you know about… / to express my sincere apologies…" [KW]
- "By the way, …" (introduces the last bullet or other news) [KW]
- "Is it possible that you can…? I would be very grateful." [KW]
- "I hope my advice is helpful to you." / "I look forward to hearing from you as soon as possible." [KW]

**Length/time**: at least 120 words, and one prompt says "about 120–150 words" (*prompt*) [TW][KW]. Spend about 20 minutes (*prompt*) [KW]. Models run 130–165 words [TW] and 151–169 words [KW].

**Tips (do / don't)**
- Answer every bullet in the order given (*pattern*) [TW][KW].
- Do not include your name or address (*prompt*) [TW]. Sign with a name only when the prompt assigns one ("You are Alex Brown") [TW].
- When suggesting or rearranging a meeting, give a concrete place and time ("sports center next Saturday at 4:00 PM"; "first week of next month… at my house") (*pattern*) [TW][KW].
- When cancelling: apologise → reason → new time (*prompt + pattern*) [TW][KW].
- Use concrete details: names, prices, apps (*pattern*); "support… with examples" (*prompt*, TW ĐỀ 10).
- Reused chunks (weather paragraph, "spicy noodles and boba tea") recur in 3–5 letters [TW] (*pattern*).

## W2: Essay

**Structure (both sources)**: intro → body 1 → body 2 → conclusion. Each body paragraph has **2 ideas**, each with an explanation and an example.
- **Advantages/disadvantages** [TW]: intro (topic is prevalent → debate → benefits and drawbacks) → B1: 2 advantages (merit → This means → For instance → additional merit → To be more specific → As a result) → B2: 2 disadvantages (primary concern → In other words → For example → Another problem → In particular → Consequently) → conclusion: pros and cons + **explicit verdict with the 2 reasons** + an "if… can…" recommendation.
- **Why? + positive/negative** [TW]: B1 gives 2 reasons ("driven by several prominent factors"), B2 weighs pro against con ("One primary advantage… Conversely…"), and the conclusion gives a verdict.
- **Discuss both views** [TW]: intro ("This essay will discuss these both views before presenting my own viewpoint") → B1 = the view the writer rejects → B2 = the view the writer supports ("Firstly / Secondly") → conclusion: "although… I am strongly convinced that…" + "Nevertheless, instead of…" compromise.
- **Discuss** [KW]: intro (heated debate → "more pain than gain" → "fascinated by… apprehensive") → B1 "On the one hand… First… Second…" → B2 "On the other hand, I share the concerns… From an X perspective…" (two perspectives) → one-sentence conclusion.
- **Causes–solutions / consequences** [KW]: B1 "the most important factor" + "Another contributing factor" → B2 "First, the government should… Second, the government should focus on investing in…" (or consequences: First / Second / Finally) → conclusion "mainly caused by A and B… can be solved by…".
- **Partly agree** [KW]: intro ends "I partly agree with the statement for the following reasons" → benefits paragraph → concerns paragraph → the standard discuss conclusion.

**Template phrases**
- "It is widely claimed that X has become increasingly prevalent among the general public in recent years." [TW]
- "As society continues to evolve, this trend has sparked considerable public debate regarding its overall impact." [TW]
- "While this issue offers a number of benefits, there are also several drawbacks involved." [TW]
- "X has an array of advantages. One of the main merits is that…" / "An additional positive aspect is…" [TW]
- "Despite the aforementioned benefits, there are also several potential downsides associated with…" [TW]
- "Nevertheless, in my view the disadvantages pale in comparison with the advantages because of…" [TW]
- "Therefore, if <actor> can…, society can fully harness…" [TW]
- "On the one hand, there are several compelling explanations behind the belief that…" [TW]
- "In conclusion, although it is believed that…, I am strongly convinced that…" [TW]
- "It is widely believed that X has become a heated debate in our society in recent years." [KW]
- "Personally I can understand the two opposing points of view; I am both fascinated by X and apprehensive about its possible negative effects." [KW]
- "On the one hand, X should bring some obvious benefits. First, X is clearly able to…" [KW]
- "<Place> is a typical example." / "It is true that…" [KW]
- "On the other hand, I share the concerns of people who believe that X has certain disadvantages." [KW]
- "In conclusion, while X is no doubt good for our society, its potential risks should not be ignored." [KW]
- "…some of the main causes of this problem will be discussed before the most important solutions are drawn." [KW]

**Length/time**: at least 250 words (*prompt*) [TW][KW]; the 40-minute limit is not stated in these files. Models run 305–378 words [TW] and 255–370 words, mostly 270–320 [KW].

**Tips (do / don't)**
- "Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience." (*prompt*) [TW]
- Marking criteria: Task Fulfillment, Organization, Vocabulary, Grammar (*prompt*) [TW][KW].
- Give each body idea an explanation and a named example (Phu Quoc, Korea/BTS, Thomas Edison, Facebook) (*pattern*) [TW][KW].
- In opinion essays, state your view in both the introduction and the conclusion (*pattern*) [TW][KW].

## S2: Solution discussion

[KS] gives about 17 situations with 3 options each (gift for sister, plan after graduation, study place, pet, part-time job, and others). **No model answers or guidance, including nothing on rejecting the other two options.**

## S3: Topic development

**Structure** [KS]: opening position sentence → "The first thing…" → "The second thing…" (→ "The third…") → "The last thing I want to say is…". Each point gives a reason, an elaboration and a result. Mind-map prompts cover **all the given branches and then one extra own idea as the last point** (health: diet, exercise, relaxing + "good relationships with family"; fast food: time, money, taste + "convenient for people who don't know how to cook"). There is no summary sentence at the end. Follow-up answers give an opinion and 1–2 reasons.

**Template phrases**
- "To be honest, I have to say that X will bring some obvious benefits." [KS]
- "…I totally agree with the statement that…" [KS]
- "The first / second / last thing I want to say is that…" [KS]
- "X is clearly able to…" / "It is true that…" / "Thanks to this, …" / "Besides, …" [KS]
- Follow-ups: "I completely agree with the view that…" / "I think…" [KS]

**Length/time**: monologues run 127–250 words (mostly 150–180); follow-up answers run about 40–45 words (2–3 sentences) [KS].

**Tips**: cover every branch plus your own idea; back each point with a reason or example (*pattern*) [KS].

## General

- No file says "don't memorise essays". All three files instead model memorised templates and reusable content [TW][KW][KS].

## Conflicts / differences between sources

1. **W1 layout**: [TW] uses 4–6 separate paragraphs; [KW] writes one unbroken block.
2. **W1 informal greeting and sign-off**: [TW] "Hi <Name>," and "All the best,"; [KW] "Dear <Name>," and "Love," or "Hugs,".
3. **W1 openers and closers** are completely different (see the W1 phrases).
4. **Contractions in formal letters**: [TW] "I'm writing in response…"; [KW] "I am writing this letter…", though its boss letter has "I don't have enough money".
5. **Formal sign-off**: [TW] picks Yours sincerely or Yours faithfully by reader; [KW] always uses "Sincerely,".
6. **W2 introduction**: [TW] "widely claimed… increasingly prevalent / a pressing concern"; [KW] "widely believed… a heated debate… more pain than gain".
7. **W2 verdict**: [TW] conclusions state which side outweighs and why. [KW] discuss and partly-agree conclusions never pick a side ("no doubt good… risks should not be ignored"), and the partly-agree conclusion does not repeat "partly agree".
8. **W2 length and level**: [TW] runs 305–378 words with C1 vocabulary ("pale in comparison", "harness"); [KW] runs 255–370 words in simpler B1–B2 language.
9. **Stance in two-sided essays**: [TW] puts the rejected view in B1, the supported view in B2, then a compromise. [KW] "partly agree" lists benefits and then concerns, with no stated stance.
10. **[KW] contradicts itself**: its last two essays (nuclear family, equal partnership) drop its own template ("In recent years…", "First and foremost", "In my humble opinion") and give a clear verdict.
11. **S1/S2**: none of these files gives guidance, so they cannot settle whether S2 must reject the other options.

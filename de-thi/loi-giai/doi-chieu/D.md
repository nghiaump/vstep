# Rulebook D: W2 essay course and sample essays

## 1. Files
- **[W2T]** WRITING_TASK_2_THEO_Dạng_.txt: a W2 course by essay type: 8 fill-in templates (all 4 paragraphs, PEER bodies) and about 100 models. Lines 1–472 repeat at 479–909. No prose tips. (L = line.)
- **[OS]** ONLINE_SHOPPING-_by_VSTEP_SV.txt: an advantages/disadvantages essay with an idea outline, by "VSTEP SV".
- **[PCG]** PLAYING_COMPUTER_GAMES.txt: a positive/negative impacts essay (the prompt also asks for solutions), by "VSTEP SV".
- **[BD]** Brain_Drain_by_Vstep_SV.txt: a causes, effects and solutions essay with 9 short paragraphs.
- **[TWC]** Travel_with_companion.txt: a preference/opinion essay with an idea outline.

## 2. W1, S1, S2, S3
None of my files contain material for these parts.

## 3. General (W2)
- **Structure**: every template is "(4 Paragraphs)": Intro, Body 1, Body 2, Conclusion [W2T]. Each idea is a **PEER**: Point, Explain, Example, Result [W2T L11–28].
- **Length/time**: the prompts say "spend about 40 minutes" and "at least 250 words" [W2T L917, 1466; PCG; TWC]. One prompt adds "Write an essay to an educated reader" [TWC; W2T L934].
- **Tips**: the Example slot is written as "[general example]" [W2T L14, 22]. Each body paragraph opens with a topic sentence before its PEERs [W2T, all templates].

## 4. W2: Opinion (agree/disagree, "to what extent", "which do you prefer")
- **Structure (template A, L1–31)**:
  - Intro: paraphrase, then concession, then opinion.
  - Body 1: the opposite view, **1 PEER**.
  - Body 2: your view, **2 PEERs**.
  - Conclusion: concession, restated opinion, and 2 reasons that "outweigh" the other side.
- **Structure (template B, L479–509)**: same order. The intro adds a roadmap sentence. The conclusion is a Summation plus a "Final Remark" that predicts future implications.
- **Opinion rule**: state it in the intro, open Body 2 with it, and restate it in the conclusion [W2T].
- **Variant (models)**: "I completely agree/disagree…" in the intro. Both bodies then support your side and there is no concession paragraph [W2T L195–199, 217–228, 257–260, 315–327].
- **Template phrases** [W2T]:
  1. "It is widely believed that [paraphrase the topic]." (L3)
  2. "While I acknowledge that [the opposite viewpoint], I believe that [your opinion]." (L3)
  3. "The following paragraphs will shed light on both perspectives before concluding with my stance." (L481)
  4. "There are several reasons why some people support the idea that + [idea]." (L5)
  5. "Admittedly, [Point]." / "Admittedly, it is understandable why some people argue that [point]." (L12, 483)
  6. PEER signposts: "This is because…" / "For example…" / "As a result…" / "Firstly… Furthermore… Consequently… Therefore…" (L13–28)
  7. "Nevertheless, I am convinced that [your opinion]." (L18)
  8. "Despite the above arguments, I firmly believe that + [your view]." (L7, 490)
  9. "To begin with…" / "Another compelling reason is that…" / "Take [example] as an illustration." (L492–499)
  10. "In conclusion, although I acknowledge that [the opposite viewpoint], I firmly believe that [restate your opinion]. This is mainly because [reason 1] and [reason 2] outweigh [the opposite argument]." (L31)
  11. "To conclude, while [opposing view] may have some merits, I believe that [your position] is more justifiable." (L10)
  12. "Looking ahead, (predict future implications…)" (L508)
  13. From the models: "While I agree that…, I would argue that…" / "That being said, I do believe…" / "To summarize, although…, I strongly believe…" (L44, 205, 94)
- **Length**: the PEER-style models run 286–308 words (L918–941). Older models run 189–274 words, and several are under 250 (L217–228: 189; L195–199: 203).

## 5. W2: Discussion of both views + opinion
- **Structure (L942–979)**:
  - Intro: view A, then view B, then your position.
  - Body 1: View 1, **1 PEER**.
  - Body 2: View 2 plus your opinion, **2 PEERs**, closed by an evaluative sentence.
  - Conclusion: restate the discussion, then a "Final Thought" recommendation.
- **Opinion rule**: state it in the intro, again in Body 2 (opening and closing sentence), and in the conclusion. Some models give the opinion only at the end of Body 2 ("In my view…", "From my perspective…") plus the intro and conclusion [W2T L1180, 1206, 1221, 1237].
- **Template phrases** [W2T]:
  1. "It is often argued that (view A). However, others hold the opposite belief that (view B)." (L944–945)
  2. "Although both sides present compelling arguments, I tend to side with (view A or view B) for several reasons." (L946)
  3. "To begin with, there are several reasons why some people are in favour of [View 1]." (L951)
  4. "One major reason is that…" / "This is largely because…" (L952–953)
  5. "Turning to the opposing viewpoint, I believe…" (L958)
  6. "The primary reason is that…" / "Another convincing argument is that…" (L960, 965)
  7. "From my point of view, while (acknowledge positives in view B), it is overshadowed by (…)." (L969)
  8. "In conclusion, although both (view A) and (view B) offer certain benefits/drawbacks, I am convinced that (your preferred view) holds more validity in the long run." (L974)
  9. "It is recommended that further measures/investigations be taken to maximize the advantages of (your position) while minimizing any drawbacks." (L977)
  10. Model intro: "People have different views about whether X or Y. While some individuals argue that X, I personally believe that Y." (L1000ff)
  11. "On the one hand, there are several explanations behind the belief that…" / "…there are compelling reasons why some people believe that…" (L1002, 1257)
  12. "On the other hand, I firmly hold the view that…" / "…I strongly believe that…" (L1004, 1259)
  13. "In conclusion, although…, it seems to me that…" (L1006ff)
- **Length**: models run 255–360 words. The newer PEER models run 317–336 words (L1255–1289).

## 6. W2: Advantages/disadvantages ("do the advantages outweigh…")
- **Structure (L1291–1320)**:
  - Intro: the topic is common, then you concede the disadvantages, then you state that the advantages are bigger.
  - Body 1: Disadvantages, **1 PEER**.
  - Body 2: Advantages, **2–3 PEERs** ("Two/Three PEERs").
  - Conclusion: restate your position, then a Recommendation.
- **Opinion rule**: the intro and conclusion take a side (by default, advantages win). The models flip the sides when the disadvantages win (L1336–1376). If the prompt only says "discuss the advantages and disadvantages", the model stays neutral: "This essay will discuss both the advantages and disadvantages of…", and the conclusion is balanced plus advice (L1488–1491).
- **Template phrases** [W2T]:
  1. "[Topic] has become increasingly common in modern society." (L1293)
  2. "While I acknowledge that [the topic] has certain disadvantages, I personally believe that these drawbacks pale in comparison with the advantages it offers." (L1293)
  3. "Admittedly, [the topic] may bring about several disadvantages." (L1295)
  4. "One notable drawback is that…" / "A typical example of this is…" (L1296–1298)
  5. "On the positive side, I believe that…" (L1302)
  6. "Another important advantage is that…" (L1309)
  7. "In conclusion, although (topic) may bring certain disadvantages, I maintain that its advantages, namely (advantages), prevail." (L1315)
  8. "To maximize benefits and reduce downsides, appropriate strategies such as (solution/measure) should be implemented." (L1318)
  9. From the models: "the most important merit is that…" / "An additional benefit is that…" / "On the other hand, despite the aforementioned benefits, I firmly believe that…" / "One primary concern is…" / "To summarize, while…, I am of the opinion that…" (L1338–1343)
- **Length**: older models run 218–234 words, under 250 (L1325–1343). Newer models run 300–316 words (L1467–1491).

## 7. W2: Cause–Solution
- **Structure (L1493–1523)**:
  - Intro: background, then a thesis giving one cause and two solutions.
  - Body 1: the cause, **1 PEER**.
  - Body 2: solutions, **2 PEERs**.
  - Conclusion: cause, then the 2 solutions, then a call for joint action.
- **Opinion rule**: no opinion statement is required.
- **Template phrases** [W2T]:
  1. "It is commonly thought that [issue]." OR "It is widely acknowledged that [issue] has become an increasingly serious problem in modern society." (L1496–1498)
  2. "This essay will examine the primary cause of this issue and propose two effective solutions to address it." (L1500)
  3. "One of the primary causes of [issue] is [cause]." / "This mainly stems from the fact that…" / "A clear example of this is…" (L1504–1506)
  4. "To address this issue, a number of practical solutions should be adopted." (L1510)
  5. "The first measure is to…" / "This can help…" / "Another effective solution is to…" / "This would enable…" (L1512–1518)
  6. "In conclusion, although [issue] mainly stems from [main cause], it can be effectively addressed through [solution 1] and [solution 2]. Therefore, both governments and individuals should work together to resolve this problem." (L1523)
  7. From the models: "The issue is ascribed to a myriad of explanations, but the most likely one is that…" / "In truth, some imperative measures ought to be taken to…" (L1614–1617)
- **Length**: the Brain drain model is 305 words and follows the template (L1727–1732). Older models run about 223 words.

## 8. W2: Problem–Solution
- **Structure (L1525–1554)**: same shape as Cause–Solution. Body 1 is one problem, **1 PEER**. Body 2 is solutions, **2 PEERs**. No opinion is required.
- **Template phrases** [W2T]:
  1. "It is commonly believed that [issue] is becoming a pressing concern worldwide." (L1530)
  2. "This essay will examine the main problem associated with this issue and propose two effective solutions to address it." (L1532)
  3. "One of the most significant problems caused by [issue] is [problem]." (L1535)
  4. "In order to overcome this problem, a range of effective measures should be introduced." (L1541)
  5. "One possible solution is to…" / "Equally important, …" / "To give an example, …" / "Accordingly, …" (L1543–1551)
  6. "…it can be effectively addressed through [solution 1] and [solution 2]. Therefore, joint efforts from governments, organizations, and individuals are essential…" (L1554)
  7. From the model: "To tackle these issues, several effective measures should be implemented." (L1743)
- **Length**: the model is 323 words and puts 2 problems in Body 1 (L1739–1745).

## 9. W2: Cause–Effect
- **Structure (L1556–1592)**: Body 1 is causes, **2 PEERs**. Body 2 is effects, **2 PEERs**. No opinion is required.
- **Template phrases** [W2T]:
  1. "It is commonly believed that [issue] has become more prevalent in recent years." (L1561)
  2. "This essay will examine the main causes of this issue and discuss its major effects." (L1563)
  3. "Several factors have contributed to the increasing prevalence of [issue]." (L1566)
  4. "In addition to its causes, [issue] can lead to a variety of significant consequences." (L1579)
  5. "One immediate effect is that…" / "An equally important consequence is that…" / "Ultimately, …" (L1581–1589)
  6. "In conclusion, [issue] can be attributed to factors such as [Cause 1] and [Cause 2], and it may lead to significant consequences including [Effect 1] and [Effect 2]." (L1592)
  7. From the models: "It is possibly said that this trend may bring some undesirable impacts, such as…" (L1602)
- **Length**: models run 234 and 388 words (L1596–1606, L1734–1737).

## 10. W2: Two-part question (for example, "Why? Is it positive or negative?")
- **Structure (L1753–1788)**:
  - Intro: background, then a thesis that previews Q1 and gives your opinion on Q2.
  - Body 1: answers Q1, **2 PEERs**.
  - Body 2: answers Q2, **2 PEERs**, with an optional concession.
  - Conclusion: the reasons, then your restated opinion.
- **Opinion rule**: give it in the thesis, the Body 2 topic sentence and the conclusion.
- **Template phrases** [W2T]:
  1. "It is widely acknowledged that [issue] has become increasingly common in modern society." (L1756)
  2. "This essay will examine [Question 1] and argue that [your opinion on Question 2]." (L1758)
  3. "There are several reasons why [issue] occurs." (L1761)
  4. "I believe that [positive/negative opinion]." / "One major advantage/disadvantage is…" (L1774–1776)
  5. "If necessary: Admittedly, [opposite viewpoint]. However, [why your opinion is stronger]." (L1785)
  6. "In conclusion, [issue] mainly occurs because of [Reason 1] and [Reason 2]. Although [brief concession if needed], I believe that [restate your opinion] because [main benefits/reasons]." (L1788)
  7. From the models: "This essay aims to explore the reasons behind… and explain why I consider it to be a positive development overall." / "This trend can be attributed to several factors." / "This trend is, in my view, a positive development." (L1392, 1634, 1639)

## 11. Sample essays ([OS], [PCG], [TWC], [BD])
- **Shared "VSTEP SV" frame [OS, PCG, TWC]**: 4 paragraphs. Body 1 is the favoured side with 2 ideas. Body 2 is the other side with 2 ideas. Lengths: OS 324, PCG 354, TWC 337 words.
- **Frame phrases** (in order of use):
  1. "In recent years, [topic] has become a heated debate in our society. A significant portion of the population has a strong belief that…"
  2. "In my humble opinion, I think its cons could never overshadow its pros." [OS, PCG]
  3. "This essay will discuss several drawbacks as well as some benefits of this matter."
  4. "First and foremost, people should recognize that…" / "A very important main point to consider is that…"
  5. "To illustrate this point, I would like to mention the fact that…" / "Another noteworthy point I want to make is that…"
  6. "On the other hand, I share the concerns of people who believe that…" / "Another point to consider is that…"
  7. "In conclusion, the aforementioned facts have highlighted the reasons and measures of this issue… People should take advantage of the pros and minimize the cons of this matter." [OS, PCG]
- **Planning**: OS and TWC start with an outline of ideas, each with a short explanation in brackets. The OS outline is 2 advantages and 2 disadvantages. The TWC outline is 3 body points, but the essay uses 2 of them.
- **Opinion**: OS and PCG give it in the intro only ("humble opinion"). TWC says "I partly agree with the former idea", and its conclusion is neutral ("both benefits and drawbacks").
- **[PCG]**: the prompt asks "what can be done to minimize the bad effects", but the essay has no solutions section.
- **[BD]**: 444 words in 9 paragraphs. Order: causes (salary, quality of life, research funding), then consequences, then solutions ("Firstly / Second / Improving the quality of life: / Third"), then 2 closing paragraphs. There is no thesis statement and no stated opinion.

## 12. Conflicts / differences between sources
1. **Intro**: [W2T] uses "It is widely believed that… While I acknowledge…, I believe…". [OS/PCG/TWC] use "…a heated debate in our society… In my humble opinion…".
2. **Body order and balance**: [W2T] gives the opposing side first (1 PEER) and your side second (2 PEERs). [OS/PCG/TWC] give the favoured side first, then "I share the concerns of…", with 2 ideas each.
3. **Conclusion**: [W2T] restates a clear opinion. [OS/PCG] speak of "reasons and measures" the essay never discussed. [TWC] is neutral, although its intro takes a side.
4. **Same topic, different structure**: Travel companion is a firm 4-paragraph PEER opinion essay of 303 words in [W2T L934–941], but hedged and balanced in [TWC]. Brain drain has 4 paragraphs, 1 cause and 2 solutions in [W2T L1727], but 9 paragraphs, 3 causes, effects and 3–4 solutions in [BD].
5. **The two opinion templates differ**: the conclusion ends with "outweigh…" in template A but with "Looking ahead…" in template B. Only template B has an intro roadmap sentence [W2T].
6. **Discussion position phrase**: the template says "I tend to side with…". The models say "I personally/firmly believe" or "it seems to me" [W2T].
7. **Idea counts**: the templates say 1 PEER for the concession, cause or problem paragraph, but many models put 2 ideas there (L53, 61, 1020, 1614–1615, 1741) [W2T].
8. **Word count**: the prompts require 250 words or more, but many [W2T] models fall short (189–234 words). Only the newer PEER models reach about 290–340.
9. **Conclusion signal**: "In conclusion," in the templates, but often "To summarize," in the models [W2T].
10. **Opinion in non-opinion types**: one Cause–Effect model adds "this is a positive trend" (L1737). The Adv/Dis Tet model stays neutral, while the template picks a side (L1488) [W2T].
11. **Data glitches in [W2T]**: some prompts sit with the wrong essay. Part-time job has an essay about sharing life online (L1466). University vs job has one about city problems (L1738). Wild animals has one about childhood (L1241). L464 is a prompt with no essay. L1325 garbles the "pale in comparison" logic.

# Rulebook E: B1 letter course + B2 30-day handbook

**Tags:** [B1] = B1_Writing_task_1.txt · [B2] = VSTEP_B2_So_tay_tu_hoc_30_ngay.txt

## 1. Files
- [B1] Notes from an informal-letter class: 10 prompts with a fixed frame, many student paragraphs with band scores (mostly 4.0–6.0) and word counts, and 3 formal letters at the end. It has no W2 or Speaking material.
- [B2] A B2 handbook compiled from 11 sources: W1 informal and formal frames, six W2 frames using PEER, Speaking Parts 1–3, a 30-day plan and checklists.

## 2. W1: rules for all letter types
**Structure**
- Number the questions in the prompt and write one body paragraph per question, with at least 3 sentences: sentence 1 answers directly, sentences 2–3 give a reason, detail or example. The handbook calls this "a practice rule, not a hard law" [B2]
- [B1] order: Dear X → opening → all answers in **one** body paragraph → closing formula → Best wishes, Name [B1]

**Register** [B2]
| | Informal (friend/relative) | Formal (organisation, manager, stranger) |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting | "Dear/Hi + first name," | "Dear Mr./Ms. + surname," / "Dear Sir or Madam," |
| Sign-off | "Best wishes," / "All the best," / "Love," (very close) | "Yours sincerely," (name known) · "Yours faithfully," (name unknown) |
| Style | contractions, phrasal verbs, "well, actually, anyway, by the way" | **no contractions**; "I would appreciate it if…", "Please do not hesitate to…" |
- The recipient decides the register: friend/Sam/Laura means informal; manager, director, customer, center or Mr./Mrs. means formal. Writing "Hi James!" to a museum director loses marks [B2]
- Put a comma after "Dear …," [B2]

**Length/time**: at least 120 words, target 150–170. Spend 3 min reading and numbering, 3 min on notes, 11 min writing and 3 min checking; never more than 20 min in total [B2]. [B1] gives no target; its samples run 115–204 words [B1]

**Tips**
- Answer every question. The teacher flagged a missing answer with "(School Schedule? Overall 5.5)" [B1][B2]
- Give concrete details (date, time, place); a line like "I will go with you" is too vague [B2]
- **Paraphrase; don't copy the prompt's sentences** [B2]
- Sign a name only if the prompt gives you a role [B2]
- Rotate 2–3 variants of openings and closings [B2]
- Write in the role the prompt assigns. A letter written from the inquirer's side was marked "(Off topic)" [B1]

### W1-A. Informal reply answering a friend's questions
**Structure**
- [B1]: Dear [letter-writer's name] → greeting plus purpose → lead-in sentence restating the friend's news with an emotion → answers to Q1, Q2, Q3 → closing → Best wishes, name [B1]
- **Pronoun swap** when restating the news: You (sentence start) ⇄ I, You (mid-sentence) ⇄ me, You are ⇄ I am, your ⇄ my, I was ⇄ you were [B1]
- [B2]: opening → one paragraph per question → closing → sign-off [B2]

**Template phrases**
- "How have you been? I and my family are doing fine here and I hope your everything goes well. I am writing to answer the questions that you raised in the letter." [B1]
- "I am very happy to receive your letter, asking about [the prompt's purpose phrase]" [B1]
- "It has been so long since I last heard from you. What have you been up to lately?… Anyway, the reason I'm writing this letter to you is to answer some questions that you raised in your letter." [B1]
- "I am very pleased/ glad/ proud/ happy/ sorry to hear that…" [B1]
- "Regarding the first question, I would emphasize is that…" · "The second thing I would add is that…" · "In relation to the third question, I would mention is that…" · "Last but not least,…" [B1]
- "My writing is quite long so I got to go now and I hope to see you soon. Please send my regards to your family and take the best care of you." [B1]
- "Many thanks for your recent letter. I'm sorry I haven't been in touch for such a long time… Anyway, it's great to hear from you." [B2]
- "In terms of… To answer your question,…" · "Regarding… To tell you the truth,…" · "As for… From my point of view,…" · "Speaking of… By the way,…" [B2]
- "Well, I think that's all I wanted to share for now… Drop me a line when you have time… I'm looking forward to hearing from you soon." [B2]
- Closings to rotate: "Take good care of yourself." · "Give my love to your family. We must try and meet up soon." [B2]

**Tips**: use "haven't been in touch for such a long time" only when it is true; for a fresh letter, open with "It was great to hear from you!" [B2]. Put the direct answer in each paragraph's first sentence [B2]

### W1-B. Informal advice / invitation / apology-cancellation / favour
**Structure**: for a cancellation, state the purpose and apologise → give the reason → propose a concrete alternative day and place → apologise again and ask for a reply; the handbook calls this "standard for every cancel/reschedule prompt" [B2]. For advice (B1 prompt 8): lead-in → where to go → what to do → weather and what to bring [B1]

**Template phrases** [B2]
- "I'm writing to apologize for missing [your party], but I'm afraid I was ill / busy…"
- "I'm having a party on Friday 19th and I hope you'll be able to come." · "Could you let me know if you can come?"
- "If I were you, I would…" · "Do visit… / Don't forget to…" · "I hope my advice will be helpful."
- "would you be available next Saturday afternoon instead?" · "Once again, I'm very sorry…"

### W1-C. Formal reply / providing information
**Structure**: "Dear Mr./Ms. X / Sir or Madam," → purpose → one paragraph per question, each opened with a **different** phrase (Concerning / In terms of / Regarding / With regard to) → fixed closing → Yours sincerely/faithfully [B2]

**Template phrases** [B2]
- "I am writing in response to your request for information regarding [topic]."
- "Concerning [Q1], I would like to inform you that…" · "With regard to [Q4], I would strongly recommend that…"
- "If you require any further information or clarification, please do not hesitate to contact me. I look forward to hearing from you."
- Openings by purpose:
  - "I am writing this letter to inquire / ask about…"
  - "…to inform you that…"
  - "…to ask if I could…"
  - "…to provide you with the information you requested…"
  - "…to apologize for V-ing…"
  - "…in response to the questions you raised in your email."
- [B1] has no formal frame. Its prompt 6 (organiser replying to a student) uses the informal friend template. Its landlord replies open "Thank you for your interest in our apartment flyer." [B1]

### W1-D. Formal request / permission / course change
**Structure**: current situation → specific reason → the request → ask about procedure → thanks [B2]. A permission letter gives the reason plus a commitment about how your work will be covered [B2]

**Template phrases**
- "I would be grateful if you could let me know whether it is possible to… and what procedures I need to follow." · "Thank you for your consideration. I look forward to your reply." → "Yours faithfully," [B2]
- [B1]'s letter to a principal: "How have you been? I hope you are doing great in health." · "I am writing this letter to you because I would like to request…" · "please kindly accept…" · "I am looking forward to hearing from you soon." → "Kind regards," [B1]

### W1-E. Complaint / feedback to a business
- Complaint: describe the incident → say how dissatisfied you are → make a specific demand (refund or replacement). Opening: "I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with the service I received…" Don't use one template for every formal purpose [B2]
- [B1]'s feedback to a restaurant: "Dear Sir or Madam," · "How have you been? I hope your work and restaurant have been going fine." · "I am writing to express my gratitude for the service you offered on…" · "I also have a suggestion to make…" → "Best regards," [B1]

### W1-F. Job application [B1 only]
- Order: the post and where you saw it → your interest → experience → skills → request for more information → thanks and contact details [B1]
- "I am reaching out to you regarding the posting for the [position] I found on [site]." · "would appreciate your consideration as a candidate for the role." · "please don't hesitate to contact me at…" → "Sincerely, [full name]" [B1]

## 3. W2 (essay) [B2 only]
**Structure**: Intro (paraphrase + thesis) → Body 1 (1 PEER) → Body 2 (2 PEER) → Conclusion. PEER = Point → Explain → Example → Result. Identify the type [B2]:
- A Opinion: stance required
- B Discussion + opinion: stance required
- C Advantages/disadvantages: stance only if the prompt says "outweigh"
- D Cause–Solution and E Cause–Effect: no stance
- F Two-part question: stance on question 2

For Opinion and Discussion essays, Body 1 concedes the other side and Body 2 argues your view [B2]

**Template phrases** [B2]
- "It is widely believed that… While I acknowledge that…, I believe that…"
- "Admittedly, it is understandable why some people argue that…" · "Despite the above arguments, I firmly believe that…"
- "On the one hand, there are compelling reasons why… / On the other hand, I strongly believe that…"
- "…these drawbacks pale in comparison with/to the advantages it offers."
- "This essay will examine the main causes of this issue and propose two effective solutions / discuss its major effects."
- "One major reason is that…" · "This can be attributed to the fact that…" · "A good example of this is…" · "Consequently,…"
- "In conclusion, although…, I am convinced that…"

**Length/time**: at least 250 words, target 270–300 (40–50 / 80–90 / 110–130 / 40–50). Time: 5 min planning, 30 min writing, 5 min checking [B2]

**Tips** [B2]
- Never write the PEER labels in the essay, and vary the linkers.
- The thesis must match the bodies. Each solution must target a named cause and say who acts and how.
- Answer both parts of a two-part prompt. If it asks for experience, give that a whole paragraph.
- No new points in the conclusion.
- Tie examples to the people in the prompt; no unverifiable statistics.
- Write "This stems from **the fact that**".
- Keep one stance throughout, and don't straw-man the other side.

## 4. S1 [B2 only]
- **Structure**: Answer → Reason → Detail, 2–4 sentences per question [B2]
- **Template phrases**: "Hello, I will talk about… Now, I will talk about…" [B2]
- **Tips**: don't give a mini-essay, but "Yes, I do." with no reason loses marks [B2]

## 5. S2 [B2 only]
**Structure**: Choose → Reason 1 (headline + explanation + example) → Reason 2 → reject **both** other options (2 sentences each) → conclude with 3 adjectives [B2]

**Template phrases** [B2]
- "In this situation, among the three options, I think [A] is the best choice. I choose this option for several reasons."
- "The first reason for my choice is that… By that, I mean… For example,…"
- "Another reason is that… In other words,… For instance,…"
- "There are also some reasons why I do not choose the other two options. Regarding [B], I do not think it is the best choice because… As for [C], I do not think it is the best option either because…"
- "To sum up, out of these three options, I believe [A] is the best choice because it is [adj], [adj] and [adj]."

**Length/time**: about 1.5–2 minutes (source #7 says about 4 minutes including preparation); samples run 251–268 words [B2]

**Tips**: pick 2–3 criteria (cost, time, effectiveness, health, social, development, long-term, practicality) that fit who is in the situation. Not rejecting the other two options is an error. Note keywords during preparation. Match the people in your examples to the prompt [B2]

## 6. S3 [B2 only]
**Structure**: Opening → 3 ideas (3 given, or 2 given + 1 of your own) each with explanation and example → "To sum up" → follow-up questions answered with Answer–Reason–Example (+ Result) [B2]

**Template phrases** [B2]
- "Some people believe that [topic]. In my opinion, it is true to say so because of the following reasons."
- "Firstly, I would say that it…" · "Secondly, it is true that it…" · "Another important point is that it…"
- "To sum up, [paraphrase]. Now I will answer the follow-up questions"
- "My view is that… One important reason is… For example,… This means that… However,…"

**Length/time**: talk for about 2 minutes, then 3–5 sentences per follow-up question [B2]

## 7. General [B2]
- Use the frame as a backbone but fit the content to the prompt; don't force a frame that doesn't match.
- Task Fulfillment comes first: underline every instruction and question.
- Writing is graded on Task Fulfillment, Organization, Vocabulary and Grammar.
- In Speaking: answer directly first, use fillers ("Well, let me think…"), correct yourself lightly, don't recite.

## 8. Conflicts / differences between sources
1. **Paragraphing:** [B2] wants one paragraph per question with at least 3 sentences; [B1] puts all answers in one paragraph, often 1–2 sentences each.
2. **Copying the prompt:** [B2] says paraphrase and don't copy; [B1]'s lead-in copies the friend's sentence with pronouns swapped.
3. **Formal sign-offs:** [B2] uses only Yours faithfully or Yours sincerely. [B1] closes formal letters with "Best regards" (to "Dear Sir or Madam"), "Kind regards" and "Sincerely".
4. **Formal openings:** [B1]'s formal letters open with "How have you been?"; [B2] opens with the purpose.
5. **"Long time" openings:** [B1] uses "It has been so long since I last heard from you" every time; [B2] says use it only when true.
6. **Signing a name:** [B1] always signs one; [B2] says only when the prompt gives you a role.
7. **Word count:** [B2] targets 150–170; [B1] has no target and scores 120–135-word samples at 4.5–5.0.
8. **Register for the organiser role:** [B1] replies to a student with the informal "Best wishes" template; [B2] ties register to the recipient.
9. **Closing lines:** [B1] uses one fixed closing ("My writing is quite long so I got to go now"); [B2] says rotate variants.
